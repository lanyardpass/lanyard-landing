A blockout date sounds binary. The park either lets your pass in that day, or it doesn't. Then you actually maintain the corpus for three operators and a dozen tiers, and the edges start showing up.

There's the park that's open but blocked for your tier while the park next door isn't, which means "am I blocked out today" is a per-park question, not a per-pass question. There's the tier that's blocked for the morning but honored after four, which is a shape our data model had to learn to hold without lying in either direction. And there's the date that was blocked when you bought the pass and quietly un-blocked later, which is a gift, but only if something tells you.

The app's rule is that we store what the operator publishes, retain what they used to publish, and never average the two. History is history and today is today.

None of this is glamorous. All of it is the difference between an app that says "probably fine" and one that says "go."
